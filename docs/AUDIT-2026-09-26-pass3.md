## Findings, ordered by damage if said out loud in the interview

### 1. The headline divides two numbers that do not measure the same thing. (Severity: highest)

The arithmetic is right: 20M / 52 = 384,615 opportunities a week; 1,200 h x 3,600 / 384,615 = **11.2 s**;
10,000 h x 3,600 / (20M / 12) = **21.6 s**. The headcount-independence claim is also right (N cancels in
(H/N)/(O/N)). What is wrong is the meaning:

- The 1,200 hours are saved by **pre-meeting briefings** ("AI-generated briefings in their inboxes ahead
  of client meetings"). The 10,000 hours are the Chief Data and Analytics Officer's figure for US advisors
  "turning to AI to prepare for client meetings", which is all AI tooling, not STAAT alone. Neither is a
  per-opportunity benefit. Nobody claims an opportunity is supposed to save preparation time.
- So "11 to 22 seconds of saved preparation per opportunity" is dimensionally sound and semantically
  empty. It says nothing about conversion, which is the argument it is used to open.
- Said to the person who built the opportunity engine, the first sentence of the PRD reads as "your
  product returns eleven seconds per output." That breaks **D-58** (never argue from an asserted weakness
  in the interviewer's own product) in the one sentence most likely to be quoted back.
- Because the source says **more than** 20M, both endpoints are **ceilings**, not a range around a point.

**Fix.** Do not divide. Put the two published measures side by side and name what they are: a **volume**
metric (20M+, up about 50%) and an **input** metric (preparation time). The number UBS does not publish is
the **conversion** from opportunity to documented, approved client action. That is the whole thesis and it
needs no ratio. If the division survives anywhere, it is a footnote, stated as "at most", with the scope
mismatch named.

### 2. The recipient counter counts the wrong unit, and probably at the wrong scope. (High)

FINRA Rule 2210(a)(2) and (a)(5) count **retail investors**, per **communication**, **firm-wide**, within
**any** 30 calendar-day period. The brief's demo beat is "batch the same note across **26 households**"
and BUILD-SPEC is described as testing a 24/25/26 boundary. Two errors a compliance officer sees in
seconds:

- A household is not a retail investor. A married couple is two. Thirteen two-person households already
  cross the line; 26 households may be 40+ recipients.
- The counter is presented as the **advisor's**. If 300 advisors send the same template to one client each,
  it is a retail communication, whatever any one advisor's counter shows.

A trailing 30-day count evaluated at each send is sufficient (any window with more than 25 distributions
ends on some send), so the mechanism is fine once the unit and scope are fixed.

**Fix.** FR-16: count distinct **retail investors** (Person nodes, excluding institutional investors under
2210(a)(4)) per communication, **across all advisors**, trailing 30 calendar days, evaluated at each send.
Demo: the 26th **person**, shown with a household of two tipping it. Boundary tests on persons, plus a
two-advisor test.

### 4. Two regulatory citations say more than the rules do. (Medium to high, in front of compliance)

- **17a-3(a)(35)** is described as "the record of the retail customer's investment profile and the
  recommendation made." The rule requires a record of **all information collected from and provided to the
  retail customer pursuant to Rule 15l-1**, plus the identity of the responsible associated person. The SEC
  did not require a record of the basis for each recommendation. V1's exit criterion "LRC confirms the
  rationale populates the 17a-3(a)(35) record" overclaims; say "supports".
- **Reg BI Care, "precisely"**: the row puts "reasonably available alternatives" inside a paraphrase of
  15l-1(a)(2)(ii). That phrase is from the **adopting release** (Release 34-86031, 2019), not the rule text.
  The rule's three limbs are (A) risks, rewards and costs, with a reasonable basis that the recommendation
  could be in the best interest of at least some retail customers; (B) a reasonable basis for **this**
  customer based on the investment profile, **without placing the firm's interest ahead**; (C) a series of
  recommended transactions not excessive. The row omits (C) and the "ahead of" language.

### 5. What a wealth-management compliance officer would look for and not find. (Medium)

- **Advisers Act fiduciary duty** (SEC 2019 interpretation) for advisory-capacity recommendations. The
  dual-registrant framing covers only the marketing rule; Reg BI does not reach advisory accounts.
- That the **marketing rule mostly excludes one-on-one communications** from "advertisement" (hypothetical
  performance aside), which strengthens consequence 1 in §7.2 on the advisory side.
- The **current** projection prohibition, FINRA 2210(d)(1)(F), which is what the firm-wide block actually
  satisfies today.
- **Form CRS**; **Advisers Act Rule 204-2** records for the advisory side; **FINRA 2241** if CIO research is
  redistributed as evidence.
- The **SEC predictive data analytics conflicts proposal**, withdrawn June 2025. Expect the question.

### 6. SR 26-2 is overstated by one clause. (Medium)

CONFIRMED at search level: Fed, OCC and FDIC, 17 April 2026, replaces SR 11-7, places generative and
agentic AI **outside scope**. But the PRD says it "expects a separate governance framework." Reported
text directs institutions to apply their **existing** risk management and governance practices to
out-of-scope systems, and the agencies plan an RFI on AI. Also it is bank supervisory guidance; it reaches
UBS's US bank entities and intermediate holding company, not the broker-dealer as such.

### 7. The PRD contradicts itself in two places the hiring manager will read closely. (Medium)

- §5.1: "The primary worked example is a **life-event** path, because that is the dominant class upstream."
  The example is an **ExternalEvent** (property sale), and "dominant class upstream" is unsourced.
- §5.3: "Constraint evaluation, **ranking** ... are deterministic code, not model calls", then "The model
  **ranks** and explains within that space." Pick one; deterministic ranking matches D-72's forbidden
  import on `lib/ranking`.

### 8. The 20M "cross-check" compares two different metrics. (Low to medium)

**opportunities**". 13M x 1.5 = 19.5M is suggestive, not confirmation. Say "consistent with", not
"internally cross-checked".
